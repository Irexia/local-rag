SOLAR PANEL INSTALLATION AND MAINTENANCE GUIDE
Version 2.4 — Department of Energy Systems
==========================================

1. SITE ASSESSMENT

Before installing any solar panel array, a thorough site assessment
must be completed. The assessment covers the following:

- Roof orientation: Panels produce up to 25% more energy when facing
  true south (in the northern hemisphere) or true north (southern
  hemisphere). East-west orientations lose approximately 10-15% in
  annual yield.

- Tilt angle: The optimal tilt angle equals the latitude of the
  installation site. For a site at 40 degrees north latitude, the
  panel should be tilted at 40 degrees from horizontal. A seasonal
  adjustment of plus or minus 15 degrees can increase annual output
  by 3-5%.

- Shading analysis: Even partial shading on a single cell can reduce
  the output of an entire string by up to 80%. A shading study using
  a solace sensor or PV*SOL software must be performed at three
  times of day (9 AM, noon, 3 PM) across equinox and solstice dates.

- Structural load: Residential roofs must support a dead load of
  at least 30 kg per square meter for the mounting hardware and
  panels, plus a live load of 1.5 kN per square meter for snow
  regions. A licensed structural engineer must sign off before
  mounting.

2. PANEL SELECTION

Panels are rated in watts-peak (Wp). A typical residential system
ranges from 3 kW to 10 kW. For a 6 kW system at a site with 4.2
peak sun hours per day, the expected daily generation is
6,000 W x 4.2 h = 25.2 kWh per day, before system losses.

System losses to account for:
- Inverter efficiency loss: 5-8%
- Wiring and connector losses: 2-3%
- Soiling losses (dust, bird droppings): 3-5% in arid regions,
  1-2% in moderate climates
- Temperature derating: approximately 0.35% per degree Celsius
  above 25 degrees ambient
- Mismatch losses: 1-3%
- Total expected system loss: 12-20%

Therefore a 6 kW system in a moderate climate should realistically
produce between 20 and 22 kWh per day.

3. INSTALLATION PROCEDURE

Step 1: Mount the racking system to the roof structure using
  pre-drilled holes sealed with Sikaflex 291 or equivalent
  construction adhesive and 16g x 100mm screws.

Step 2: Attach the MC4 connectors to the panel junction boxes.
  Ensure the positive (+) and negative (-) leads are correctly
  identified. A reverse polarity connection can damage the inverter.

Step 3: Run DC cabling (minimum 6 mm² cross-section for runs under
  20 metres) through a waterproof conduit from the panel array to
  the inverter location.

Step 4: Connect the inverter to the main switchboard at the
  distribution board. The inverter output cable must be minimum
  10 mm² and fused at 80% of the inverter rated current.

Step 5: Ground the frame of each panel and the inverter chassis to
  the earth electrode. Grounding wire must be minimum 4 mm²
  green/yellow insulated cable.

Step 6: Commission the system. Power on the inverter, verify DC
  input voltage is within the inverter MPPT range, and confirm AC
  output at the distribution board.

4. MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE

- Monthly: Visual inspection for physical damage, loose mounting
  hardware, and abnormal inverter error codes.
- Quarterly: Clean panels with a soft brush and deionised water.
  Never use abrasive cleaners or pressure washers above 40 bar,
  as these can crack the tempered glass cover.
- Annually: Inspect all DC and AC connections for corrosion or
  terminal loosening. Check inverter firmware for updates.
- Every 5 years: Replace all DC connectors (MC4) as a precaution,
  as UV degradation of the polymer housing can lead to arcing.

5. COMMON FAULT CODES

Code  F-001: Grid voltage out of range. Check utility connection.
Code  F-002: DC input overvoltage. Verify panel string voltage
            does not exceed inverter maximum DC voltage (typically
            600V or 1000V depending on model).
Code  F-003: Inverter over-temperature. Ensure ventilation clearance
            of at least 15 cm on all sides.
Code  F-004: Ground fault detected. Inspect DC cabling for
            insulation damage.
Code  F-005: Communication failure between inverter and monitoring
            gateway. Check RS485 or Wi-Fi link.

RESIDENTIAL BATTERY STORAGE SYSTEMS — TECHNICAL REFERENCE
Version 1.1 — Energy Systems Division
=====================================

1. OVERVIEW

Residential battery storage systems allow homeowners to store
excess solar energy generated during the day for use during the
evening or during grid outages. Typical residential systems range
from 5 kWh to 20 kWh of usable capacity.

2. CHEMISTRY COMPARISON

Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4):
- Energy density: 90-160 Wh/kg
- Cycle life: 3,000-6,000 cycles at 80% depth of discharge
- Operating temperature: 0 to 45 degrees C
- Thermal stability: Excellent, non-flammable electrolyte
- Cost: Moderate
- Best for: Long-duration residential storage, high safety

NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt):
- Energy density: 150-220 Wh/kg
- Cycle life: 1,000-2,000 cycles at 80% depth of discharge
- Operating temperature: 0 to 45 degrees C
- Thermal stability: Moderate, requires BMS thermal management
- Cost: Lower per kWh
- Best for: EVs, shorter-duration storage

Lead-Acid (AGM / Gel):
- Energy density: 30-40 Wh/kg
- Cycle life: 300-500 cycles at 50% depth of discharge
- Operating temperature: -20 to 50 degrees C
- Thermal stability: Good
- Cost: Lowest initial, highest lifetime (due to replacement)
- Best for: Off-grid backup, sensitive users on a budget

3. BATTERY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (BMS)

The BMS is the critical safety and performance component of any
lithium battery system. It monitors and controls:

- Cell voltage: Individual cell balancing to keep all cells
  within 50 mV of each other. A drift beyond 200 mV indicates
  an aging or defective cell.
- Temperature: NTC thermistors placed on each cell. Charging
  must be halted if any cell exceeds 45 degrees C or drops
  below 0 degrees C.
- Current: Both charge and discharge current must be limited
  to the cell datasheet maximum. A typical 100 Ah LiFePO4
  cell is rated for 20A continuous discharge (2C).
- State of Charge (SOC) estimation: Uses a combination of
  voltage lookup tables, current integration (Coulomb
  counting), and periodic open-circuit voltage calibration.

4. DEPTH OF DISCHARGE AND LIFETIME

For LiFePO4 cells, the relationship between depth of discharge
(DOD) and cycle life follows approximately:

- 20% DOD: 8,000+ cycles
- 40% DOD: 5,000+ cycles
- 60% DOD: 3,000+ cycles
- 80% DOD: 2,000-3,000 cycles
- 100% DOD: 800-1,200 cycles (not recommended)

A 10 kWh system used daily at 60% DOD will deliver approximately
6 kWh per day and last around 3,000 days (8 years) before
capacity drops below 80% of rated.

5. SAFETY REQUIREMENTS

- The battery enclosure must be IP65 rated or higher for
  outdoor installation.
- Ventilation: At least 0.5 m³/min of airflow per kWh of
  installed capacity for indoor installations.
- The battery must be installed on a non-combustible surface.
- A Class D fire extinguisher must be available within 3 metres.
- The DC disconnect switch must be clearly labelled and located
  within 2 metres of the battery.
- All wiring must be rated for the maximum continuous current
  plus 25% for fault conditions.
- The installation must comply with local electrical codes
  (IEC 62619, UL 1973, or equivalent).

6. TYPICAL SYSTEM CONFIGURATION (10 kWh)

- 16 x LiFePO4 cells (3.2V nominal each) in series = 51.2V
- BMS: 16S, 100A continuous, 200A peak
- Cells: 100 Ah each, matched within 1% capacity
- Inverter/Charger: 5 kW hybrid, 51.2V input, 230V AC output
- Expected lifetime at 50% daily DOD: 10+ years

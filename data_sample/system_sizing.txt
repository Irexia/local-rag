RESIDENTIAL SOLAR AND STORAGE SIZING GUIDE
Version 1.0 — Energy Systems Division
=====================================

1. ESTIMATING HOUSEHOLD LOAD

Start from twelve months of electricity bills and calculate the
average daily consumption in kWh. Typical daily consumption is:

- Small apartment: 5-8 kWh per day
- Average home: 10-15 kWh per day
- Large home with air conditioning or an EV charger: 20-30 kWh per day

Evening load is the energy used between 6 PM and 11 PM, when solar
generation is zero. It is typically 35-45% of daily consumption.

Worked example: a home uses 14 kWh per day. At an evening share of
40%, the evening load is 5.6 kWh.

2. PEAK SUN HOURS BY REGION

- Northern Europe: 2.5-3.0 hours per day
- Central Europe and other moderate climates: 3.5-4.5 hours per day
- Mediterranean and US Southwest: 5.0-6.5 hours per day
- Equatorial regions: 4.5-5.5 hours per day

Use measured local data whenever it is available.

3. SIZING THE ARRAY

Array size in kW = daily energy target / (peak sun hours x 0.8).
The factor 0.8 reflects the upper end of the system loss range.

Worked example: 14 kWh per day at 4.2 peak sun hours gives
14 / (4.2 x 0.8) = 4.17 kW. Round up to 11 panels of 400 Wp,
which is a 4.4 kW array.

A typical modern panel is rated 400 Wp and covers about 1.9 square
metres. A 6 kW array therefore needs 15 panels and about 28.5 square
metres of roof area.

4. SIZING THE BATTERY

Usable capacity needed = evening load x 1.2. The 20% margin covers
conversion losses and ageing.

Nominal capacity = usable capacity / planned depth of discharge.

Worked example: an evening load of 5.6 kWh x 1.2 = 6.72 kWh usable.
At 60% depth of discharge the nominal capacity is 6.72 / 0.6 =
11.2 kWh.

5. BACKUP AND AUTONOMY

For backup during a grid outage, list the critical loads and add up
their power. The total simultaneous critical load must stay below the
continuous rating of the inverter.

Typical critical loads:
- Refrigerator: 150 W average
- LED lighting: 100 W
- Router and modem: 20 W
- Phone and laptop chargers: 60 W

These four loads total 330 W. Over a 12-hour outage they consume
3.96 kWh. A backup target of one day for critical loads only is
typical.

6. DEGRADATION AND REPLACEMENT

Panel output degrades by about 0.5% per year. After 25 years expect
about 87% of the original output. The design life of the panels is
25-30 years. The battery is normally replaced before the panels, and
its replacement interval depends on the depth of discharge used.
